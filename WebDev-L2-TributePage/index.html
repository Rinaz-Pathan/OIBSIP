Dr. A. P. J. Abdul Kalam

The Missile Man who taught a nation to dream.

His Story

Avul Pakir Jainulabdeen Abdul Kalam was born on 15 October 1931 in Rameswaram, a small island town in Tamil Nadu. His family was not wealthy, and as a boy he delivered newspapers to help with household expenses. He studied hard, graduated in physics, and then trained as an aerospace engineer in Chennai. Even in those early years, his curiosity about flight and rockets was clear.

Kalam began his career at DRDO and later joined ISRO, where he worked on India's space and missile programmes. He led the SLV-III project, which placed the Rohini satellite into orbit in 1980 and made India one of the few countries able to launch its own satellites. His work on missile technology later earned him the nickname "Missile Man of India". He also played a key role in the nation's science and defence achievements in the 1990s.

In 2002, Kalam became the 11th President of India. Unlike many leaders, he stayed simple and approachable in the highest office. He loved meeting students and often spoke to them about science, hard work and big dreams. This warmth is why millions of people came to call him the "People's President".

Dr. Kalam passed away on 27 July 2015 while giving a lecture to students in Shillong, doing what he loved most until the very end. He left behind not just rockets and missiles, but a generation inspired to dream bigger. I admire him because he rose from a modest background through discipline, humility and education. His life shows that where you start does not decide how far you can go.

Key Milestones

  1. 1931: Born in Rameswaram, Tamil Nadu.
  2. 1969: Joined ISRO.
  3. 1980: SLV-III successfully placed the Rohini satellite in orbit.
  4. 1997: Awarded the Bharat Ratna.
  5. 2002: Became the 11th President of India.
  6. 2015: Passed away in Shillong, while delivering a lecture.
